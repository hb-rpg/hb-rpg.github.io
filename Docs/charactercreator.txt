

First Level Characters ###
▶ CHARACTER CREATION OVERVIEW
This is an overview of the 8 Steps to create your character. Additional details of each Step are provided in the separate  sections that follow.  Review and complete all of the headings like this one with the “▶” recording the results of your rolls or selections on the Character Sheet.
If you cannot make a quick selection, roll dice as indicated for a random result. If you do not like that result, then make a selection or roll again. If you are unhappy with your selections after playing one or two sessions, discuss changing some of the selections with your GM or generate an entirely new character.
Step 1 - Ancestry, Level, Hit Die and Hit Points 
Select one of six Ancestries: Dwarf, Elf, Halfling, Human, Ixian, or Orc. 
Your character begins as a commoner at Level 0. You have 1 Hit Die and you start with 4 Hit Points.
You may also select a gender and/or gender expression for your character for flavor. 
Step 2 - Background 
Select a Background from the six groups. Backgrounds are grouped related to Classes but you are free to select from any of the groups. At 1st Level, you will be able to select a Class Your Background indicates your character’s experiences and life before starting a life of adventure. 
Your Background selection may affect your Entanglements in Step 4.   
Your Background selection may add or reduce your Starting Equipment in Step 5. 
Step 3 - Ability Scores 
Your character’s starting mental and physical potential is represented by a score of 2 to 12 for six Abilities: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Wisdom, and Charisma. You may roll to randomly generate the Ability Scores or assign preset scores from an array. 
Step 4 - Entanglements 
Your character has a past that can impact their present and future opportunities in both positive and negative ways. Your previous selection Background often affects Entanglements.
Step 5 - Starting Equipment 
Your character starts with a standard set of gear as well as a significant trinket that they inherited or acquired in the recent past. Your selection of Ancestry and Background may increase or decrease your starting gear. 
Step 6 - Languages 
Your Ancestry and Background selections determine your starting languages. This step allows you some final selections or adjustments to your character’s literacy. Unless noted otherwise, you can either choose another language to speak or the ability to read & write a language you already know how to speak.  
The common languages are Dwerg, Infernal, Istya, Kaduz, Nulya, Kelinya, Muluk, Sindar, Sulya, and Sylvan.
Step 7 - Religion 
In the default setting for Heartbreaker, your character and most folks you encounter will be polytheistic and venerate the deity that represents the issue for which they are seeking intercession. You should select a primary deity that aligns with your character’s purpose and one or two secondary deities you character would also honor regularly. 
Warlocks are an exception to this and they are typically devoted to a singular powerful entity who is jealous and does not allow the Warlock to honor or venerate anyone else. 
Step 8 - Corruption 
Magic is dangerous to both practitioners and often those in the general vicinity. If indicated by your Background, you must roll for the effect of some magical corruption that is afflicting your character.

▶ Step 1 Overview - Ancestry, Level, Hit Die and Hit Points
Ancestry is your species and cultural heritage. Roll 1d6 or choose an Ancestry from the table below. 
Each Ancestry comes with a package of standard features in the following categories: Edges, Spells, Skills, Languages, Gear, Names, and Other. If a category is not included, there is no feature.
You start at Level 0. “Level” indicates your experience and you gain new abilities at new levels. Your starting character is a commoner who has experienced a calling or catalyst to begin a life of adventure.
To facilitate quick decisions during character creation, only a brief description providing the flavor and purpose of the Edge, Spell, and Skill are provided. Full details and mechanics are intentionally left out to avoid “analysis paralysis” or confusion of rule concepts that a new player may not understand yet. Full details are provided in Ch. 6 -  Edges & Skills and Ch. 7 - Spellcasting & Spells. 
You have 1 Hit Die. “Hit Die” is abbreviated HD and is one measure of your toughness and size.  
You start with 4 Hit Points. “Hit Points” is abbreviated HP and is a measure of how much damage you can endure. 
Indicate a gender and/or gender expression for your character. There is no impact on your character in terms of game mechanics in regards to their gender.  

Edges are powerful abilities that are innate to that Ancestry and/or gained through extensive training. 
Spells are any divine, profane, or arcane powers you have been taught, discovered, or are innate to your Ancestry. 
Skills are less powerful knacks and abilities learned through practice and cultural immersion from your Ancestry. 
Languages indicate the typical spoken tongue learned by your character based on their Ancestry. Your character cannot read or write the indicated language(s) unless specifically noted or gained in a later Step.
Gear indicates items your character will have in addition to those from Step 5 - Starting Equipment. Note these items on your character sheet. Later steps may add or remove items.
Names indicate common names, bynames, and epithets for your Ancestry. A byname typically refers to a geographical origin or relationship, infamous relative, or clan. An epithet is typically a descriptive characteristic, habit, or quality. Your character may aspire to some chosen epithet or have been unwillingly assigned an undesirable one that will require some significant event or accomplishment to change.
Other is included for any other special features related to your Ancestry.

Roll of 1  - DWARF
Dwarves are hardy, independent mountain folk respected for their crafting ability skills with stone and metal. They are suspicious of outsiders but fiercely loyal to their clan and those who have earned their trust and friendship.  Dwarves often trade their products, services, and protection with nearby halfling settlements for food, beer, and craft goods that they do not manufacture for themselves.

Roll of 5 - Ixian
Ixians are humanoids of extraplanar, profane origin. You are of extraplanar origin, are descended from an extraplanar immigrant, or offspring of one of these with mixed or full fiendish blood. This heritage can be expressed by black eyes, dark red or blue skin, curved black nails, cloven hooves, a tail, bony protrusions at joints, and/or horns. 
The emergence of Ixians coincided with the fall of the human empire and several calamitous events. Ixians are regarded with suspicion if not outright aggression by most others. It is not uncommon for an Ixian to disguise their origin unless they are belligerent or powerful enough to fend off aggressors.

▶ Step 2 Overview - Background
Your Background indicates your character’s experiences and life before starting a life of adventure. Often it will provide prompts and suggested catalysts for why your character began a life of adventure.
There are six Background groups that loosely correlate to each Class which you choose later at 1st Level. You are not required to choose the Class related to your Background. Within each grouping, there are six or more specific Background choices. 
You may select a Background from any one of the six groups or roll 1d6 twice. The first roll is to determine the group. The second roll is to determine the Background within that group. Some Backgrounds (Artisan, Bureaucrat, Crafter, Laborer, and Vagabond) have additional options that you can choose from or roll a third 1d6 for a random selection.
Each Background package comes with some standard features in the following categories: Edges, Spells, Skills, Languages, Gear, Names, and Other. If a category is not included, there is no feature.
To facilitate quick decisions during character creation, only a brief description providing the flavor and purpose of the Edge, Spell, and Skill are provided. Full details and mechanics are intentionally left out to avoid “analysis paralysis” or confusion of rule concepts that a new player may not understand yet. Full details are provided in Ch. 6 -  Edges & Skills and Ch. 7 - Spellcasting & Spells. 
Edges are powerful abilities that are innate or through extensive training in the Background. Reroll or make a different selection if you already have one of the indicated Edges from a previous Step. A brief but incomplete description is provided for the Edges. See Ch. 6 - Edges & Skills for full details.
Spells are any divine, profane, or arcane powers you have been taught or discovered from your Background. A brief but incomplete description is provided for any Spells. See Ch. 7 - Spellcasting & Spells for full details.
Skills are less powerful knacks and abilities related to the Background learned through practice. A brief but incomplete description is provided for the Skills. See Ch. 6 - Edges & Skills for full details.
Gear indicates items added, changed, or removed from those indicated in Step 5 - Starting Equipment. Some of the Backgrounds reflect hardship and gear and money may be reduced or eliminated. Note these items on a separate sheet of scratch paper to resolve for when you get to Step 5. For additional information or any special characteristics of your Gear, see Ch 3 - Equipment.
Languages are included if there is some special language skill related to your character’s Background. Your character cannot read or write the indicated language(s) unless specifically noted or gained in a later Step.
Names indicate common names, bynames, and epithets related to your Background. An epithet is typically a descriptive characteristic, habit, or quality. Your character may aspire to some chosen epithet or have been unwillingly assigned an undesirable one that will require some significant event or accomplishment to change.
Other details include any special features regarding your Background. It will detail income and employment you may pursue. It will also provide requirements or suggestions regarding your Background’s effect on Entanglements that are determined in Step 4. Some Backgrounds, like Warlock, Adept, and Arcane Apprentice require you to select a Master who instructed you in the arcane mysteries.

▶ Step 3 Overview - Ability Scores
Your character’s starting mental and physical potential is represented by a score of 2 to 12 for six Abilities. Scores will increase as the character advances in Level to a maximum of score of 18. 
Most actions are resolved with an Ability Test where the target number for success is a roll lower than your relevant Ability Score. See Ch 1 - Core Mechanics, Action Resolution for more information.
Review the explanation and application of the six Abilities below. Then generate scores by rolling 3d6 (Method 1) or assign scores from a preset array of numbers (Method 2) as indicated below.



ABILITIES
STR   (STRENGTH) is your capacity to exert bodily, physical force.  It is used for lifting, throwing, breaking and to make melee attacks.  You may also use STR for ranged attacks with hand thrown weapons if it is higher than your DEX.
DEX  (DEXTERITY) is a measure of your agility, reflexes, coordination, and balance.  DEX is used to dodge attacks, move stealthily, climb, and to make ranged attacks.  You may also use DEX for melee attacks with Light weapons if it is higher than your STR.
CON  (CONSTITUTION) represents your stamina, health, and endurance.  CON is used to resist physical pain, poison, disease, and succumbing to the effects of wounds. 
INT     (INTELLIGENCE) represents your knowledge, perception, and memory.  INT is used for searching, tracking, and seeing through illusions.  With training, INT is employed to harness and project arcane power.
WIS   (WISDOM) indicates your willpower, discipline, and intuition.  WIS is used to resist charms, maintain concentration, and sense motives in others.  With training, WIS is used to evoke, influence, and control divine or diabolic powers.
CHA  (CHARISMA) is a measure of persuasiveness, eloquence, and leadership.  CHA is used to influence others, improve reaction/attitude of NPCs, and lead others.  Some fey and those with special training can employ CHA to tap and control internal sorcerous powers.


RPG Dice ###







ABILITY SCORES


Determine scores using one of the following two methods to determine your character’s Ability Scores.
Method 1

Kara Spell Caster ###
Roll 3d6 and drop the lowest number to generate a number from 2 to 12.
Record this number on a scrap sheet of paper. 
Repeat this to generate 6 numbers.
Assign each number as you desire to STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, & CHA.
Method 1 generates an average number of 8.46 but you take on the risk of having a scope of 2 to have the possibility to have a score of 12.

Example
Anita rolls the first 3d6 resulting in 2, 4, & 6. 
She drops the 2, so records 10. 
She then rolls 3, 3, and 4. Dropping 3, she records 7. 
She rolls 2, 3, and 6. Dropping 2, she records 9.  
She rolls 3, 5, and 5. Dropping 3, she records 10.
She rolls 1, 2, and 3. Dropping 1, she records 5.
Finally she rolls 5, 6, and 6 and records 12.
Anita wants her character Kara to eventually be both an Arcane and Divine Spellcaster so she assigns higher scores to INT and WIS. 
Kara’s starting Ability Scores are: 
STR: 5   DEX: 7   CON: 10   INT: 12   WIS: 10   CHA: 9
Method 2
Assign six numbers from a standard array to your character’s Abilities: 7, 8, 8, 9, 9, and 10.
Method 2 has an average of 8.5 eliminating the chance for low scores but also higher scores.

▶ Step 4 Overview - Entanglements
To determine the attitudes for each of your Entanglements, roll 5d6 and record each number as well as a 1 and 6. Assign each number to one of the seven  groups: Colleagues, Family, Local Civic Authorities, Local Religious Authorities, Master/Mentor/Lord, Neighbors/Local Inhabitants, and Shadow Groups. Descriptions of those groups are provided below in Entanglement Groups.
First, assign any number per the recommendation (if you choose) or as required by your Ancestry, Class, and/or Background. Afterwards, assign any remaining numbers to each remaining group as you choose.
The number assigned to a group indicates the general demeanor and reaction members of that group will have towards you as indicated below in Entanglements Attitudes: 1: Aggressive, 2: Hostile, 3: Negative, 4: Disinterested, 5: Positive/Receptive, and 6: Friendly/Sympathetic. 
The relationships and events from the Entanglements are the catalyst for why your character is starting a life of adventure.
After each number has been assigned to a group, use them as prompts to help you develop a three to five sentence backstory framework for your character.  This brief outline/skeleton of a backstory should incorporate at least three familial bonds, professional bonds, friendships, enmities, and/or fallout from bad decisions or events. You and the GM can use these going forward to develop relevant plot seeds and storylines in relation to any other characters and their Entanglements. 
Also, seek out the Groups that have high numbers assigned (positive attitudes) to provide you with information, rumors, a safe haven to recuperate, and other resources if needed.
Note that the attitudes are not fixed. As you adventure some may improve or deteriorate. Some may be replaced. If you move far away (over 200 miles) from your point of origin, the Attitude for all groups with “Local” in their name will change. They will typically be 3 - Negative or 4 - Disinterested. 
Also, some of the groups are broad and can be expanded by your GM to differentiate between the various deities and various Shadow Groups like smugglers, cutlists, slavers, anarchists, rebels, etc. 
Following the Entanglement sections is a last section called Other Catalysts. It provides other optional life events and occurrences that are currently fairly common in Kelinya for the past 60 years since the Second Incursion by the Ixians.  



ENTANGLEMENT GROUPS
Colleagues – These are people that you currently work with or have worked with in the last three years. They could be fellow apprentices, pages, fellow students, initiates, or members of  your gang/cohort.
Family - These could be a specific person or all of your immediate family (siblings, parents), extended family (cousins, aunts/uncles, grandparents) or a whole clan that is interrelated. 
Local Civic Authorities – These people are governing power in an area overseen by mayors, elders, chiefs, lords, councils, guilds, and nobles. Their authority will be enforced by beadles, constables, reeves, wardens, knights, and city guards.  Local Authorities is defined as a 25 mile radius area where you have spent most of your life or have been residing for the past few years.
Local Religious Authorities - These people are the spiritual authorities within a 25 mile radius of where  you have spent most of your life or have been residing for the past few years. This could be an organized priesthood or a decentralized organization of shamans, druids or holy people.  In rare instances this could also be a powerful creature like an Ixian Raver, Ixian Archon, devil, or ancient dragon that demands worship. In a theocratic society, the religious authorities govern and the local authorities are also under their direction and control. In a traditional theocracy, enforcement of their authority is through religious court magistrates and Inquisitors.
Master - This is your current or former master, teacher, mentor, lord, or employer.
Neighbors/Local Inhabitants - This group represents commoners within a 25 mile radius of where you have spent your life or have been residing for the past few years.
Shadow Groups - These are people with clandestine agendas that are typically involved in illicit activities. Examples include cultists, anarchists, rebels, press gangs, thieves guilds, secret societies, and cabals of necromancers/witches/warlocks. Criminal activity could include ritual murders, assassinations, kidnapping, slavery, extortion, bribery, and/or fencing stolen goods. Rural and smaller settlements will have only one or two shadow groups in the region but a large, urban area may have multiple Shadow Groups that will compete with each over territory if engaged in overlapping activities.

▶ Step 6 Overview - Languages
You speak one or more languages based on your Ancestry, Class and Background. 
If it was indicated, you can also read & write some of those languages. 
Unless indicated otherwise by your Class, Ancestry, or Background, you can choose or roll 1d10 for an additional language from the list below that you can only speak. 
Your Class, Ancestry, or Background may also have granted you the ability to read and write this additional language.
You can add or improve languages skills (speaking then reading & writing) as you gain levels. See Ch 5 - Advancement.




COMMON LANGUAGES OF THE  CONTINENT


1
Dwerg is spoken by Dwarves who live in remote mountains or subterranean settlements. It is also spoken by many other denizens of Underdark. The spoken form is clipped with short syllables and can sometimes sound harsh. Some dialects include taps and claps. These percussive elements are also used in short repeating phrases to communicate with drums and flashes of light across long distances or through the dark. The written form consists of runes made with vertical, horizontal, and diagonal slashes and dots that are easy to carve in wood or stone with an edged tool.
2
Infernal is spoken by Ixians and powerful extraplanar creatures from profane realm such as Ixian Ravers and Ixian Archons. Warlocks and some necromancers also learn this language through their studies. With the arrival Ixians in the northern capitals of the empire around one hundred years ago, the language was learned by many scholars and researchers. The spoken form is guttural with hard consonants. The written form consists of complex runes and pictograms. 
3
Istya is spoken by the raiding invaders from the Eastern continent who began arriving, raiding, and creating settlements around 40 years ago. The spoken form consists of quick , hard syllables with meanings changing due to tonal inflections. The written form consists of complex pictograms.
4
Kaduz is spoken by Dwarves who live in hills or in the surface settlements.  It is also commonly spoken by Halflings who reside in the hills and valleys alongside dwarven settlements. It is a similar but distinct variant related to Dwerg. Throughout the centuries it has assimilated words from the Kelinya family of languages as well as developing its own idioms often introduced by Halflings. It uses the same runic alphabet as Dwerg but has incorporated curved elements and flourishes only suitable for writing in ink.
5
Kelinya is an older, formal language used by the original human empire before it split into northern and southern territories that also subsequently fractured further into smaller independent states. Despite this, its uniformity has made it useful for communication and commerce so it is still widely used for commercial and legal documents. The Kelinya alphabet consists of simple circle, line, and slash configurations representing phonetic sounds.
6
Nulya is a dialect of Kelinya spoken primarily by Humans and Halflings inhabiting the northern portion of the Kelinya Empire that split then eventually collapsed around 80 years ago. The north has maintained a higher level of large urban settlements and the language is used and known by a larger population than all other languages on the continent. The spoken version has developed an extensive collection of idioms over the last few hundred years as well as assimilated words from Kaduz, Infernal, and Sylvan. It uses a modified and extended version of the Old Kelinya alphabet consisting of circle, line, and slash configurations representing phonetic sounds.
7
Muluk is spoken by Orcs and the nomadic barbarians from southwestern portion of the continent. The spoken form has brief sentences consisting of hard consonants and clicks. The click patterns are also used in short repeating phrases to communicate with drums across long distances or at night.
8
Sindar is an ancient language spoken by the nigh extinct High Elves who live in ancient, remote,  mountain citadels and by dragons. It is rumored that Sindar was the language used by Weut to call forth the waters from the void at the moment of creation. Sindar is also spoken by arcane researchers and Dark Elves that live in Underdark. The spoken form is melodic and flowing with meanings changing with word order and tonal inflections. The written form consists of thousands of complex pictograms.
9
Sulya is a dialect of Kelinya spoken by Human and Halfling farmers and herders of the Southern grasslands. Due to its lack of hard geographic borders, the southern portion of the old empire has experienced numerous conquests and invasions. Due to this, it has assimilated a multitude of words and idioms over the last 80 years from Muluk, Sylvan, and more recently Istya. Though not used extensively due to its decentralization, Sulya does have its own alphabet. It uses a modified and extended version of the Old Kelinya alphabet consisting of circle, line, and slash configurations representing phonetic sounds.
10
Sylvan is spoken by Elves and other forest folk who live in the primeval forests of the continent and Halfling who live in the foothills and bordering areas. Sylvan is related to Sindar but diverged over 500 years ago after an extended internecine war among elves leading to the isolation of the High Elves, the migration of the Dark Elves to Underdark and the remaining Elves congregating and settling in forests.   
Like Sindar, the spoken form is melodic and flowing with meanings changing with word order and tonal inflections but it has slowly assimilated some Kelinya based words and colorful idioms, especially through their interaction with Halflings who love wordplay and complex metaphors. The written form consists of thousands of pictograms.

▶ Step 7 Overview - Religion
In Heartbreaker, the default setting is the planet of Yrath on the continent of Kelinya. In Kelinya, most people are polytheistic. The deities are universal, abstract concepts, typically transcending Ancestry and gender.  
Commoners may favor a particular deity but will typically still revere, pray, and make offerings to other deities depending upon circumstances and the occasion as supplicants.  Rural commoners especially favor Abala, Asherah, Tubal, and Weut. In urban areas, all are equally revered. Clergy will often focus on a specific deity but still honor or at least acknowledge the other deities except for Ghoelb. 
Cultists and warlocks are the exception and typically focus on a specific deity to the exclusion of all others. They seek forbidden power from deities such as Ghoelb, Moloch or other powerful entities such as ancient evil dragons, malevolent liches, scheming Ixian Archons, or destructive Ixian Ravers. 
Below are brief descriptions of the deities, their areas of influence (portfolio), symbol, and rune. You should choose the deity your character follows or favors but if a random determinate is required, roll 1d12.
1: Abala, 2: Asherah, 3. Enoch, 4: Gestas, 5: Ghoelb, 6: Hiram, 7: Juba, 8: Kain, 9: Moloch, 10: Tubal, 11: Weut, and 12: None 
You are not required to select a specific deity but if or when you take the Theurgy 1 Edge, selecting a specific deity to venerate has benefits for casting spells from specific schools of magic.
There is also brief information regarding any special assistance they provide to adherents who are Theurgic Spellcasters. See Ch 7 - Spellcasting and Spells, Theurgic Spellcasting for more detailed information.

Juba is especially honored by women, bards, musicians, thespians, itinerant performers, elves, halflings, and humans. In religious tradition, it was Juba’s music and storytelling that charmed Ghoelb allowing the other gods to capture and imprison them. 
For Theurgic Spellcasters, Juba provides aid to followers casting Enchantment spells (magic that charms or influences others).  


Kain represents battle, conflict, and sorrow. Kain is also invoked by oathbreakers to protect them from consequences. In some religious traditions, Kain was created by Ghoelb but betrayed them in order to protect the world made by Weut and the other gods. When Kain fought Ghoelb to subdue them, their sword shattered because of the betrayal. Kain is especially honored by warriors, dwarves, orcs, and Ixians. 
For Theurgic Spellcasters, Kain provides aid to followers casting  Abjuration spells (magic that is used for protection from forces or supernatural beings).


Moloch represents darkness, revenge, death, and grief. In religious tradition, Moloch was imprisoned in a maze as a sacrifice to a minotaur. The minotaur spared Moloch and helped them escape. Like Ghoelb, Moloch is often invoked for curses against hated rivals and enemies. Moloch is the gatekeeper for the realms of the afterlife. Moloch is especially honored by warriors, barbarians, Orcs, Ixians, Minotaurs, and necromancers. Followers often tattoo a teardrop below one or both eyes. Moloch is exclusively worshiped by some warriors, cultists, and spellcasters seeking forbidden necromantic lore. 
For Theurgic Spellcasters, Moloch provides aid to followers casting Necromancy spells (magic that manipulates life force) and Illusion spells (magic that is used to trick the senses and perceptions). 


Tubal represents fire, metalworking, and crafts in general. Tubal is the dour and serious sibling of Juba. In religious tradition, Tubal forged the magic chains that were used to imprison Ghoelb. Tubal is especially revered by artisans, crafters, smiths, and dwarves. For Theurgic Spellcasters, Tubal provides aid to followers casting Evocation spells (magic that creates and manipulates energy and elements), especially those related to light, heat, and fire. 


Weut represents creativity, nature, and creation. Weut is an ancient, primeval entity who created the other gods to aid in the imprisonment of Ghoelb so that creation could begin. Taking the form of an owl, Weut created water from the void then drew up land and mountains from the calm seas. They then nested on the highest mountain to oversee the remainder of creation carried out by the other gods. Weut is especially revered by artisans, high elves, and spellcasters. For Theurgic Spellcasters, Weut provides aid to followers casting Transmutation spells (magic that changes the properties of a being, energy, or substances).

▶ Step 8 Overview - Corruption
Magic is dangerous to both practitioners and often those in the general vicinity. 
Your character only has Corruption when indicated by your Class or Background. During adventures, anyone exposed to a magic mishap can receive Corruption.
To determine Corruption, roll two dice. Roll a d20 for the Corruption Effect and a d6 for the Corruption Severity.

You can wear gloves that need to be replaced weekly to protect possessions you touch. Expensive alkaline substances can be purchased (20 coins/week) and if applied regularly can mitigate the effects. Your touch causes 1 point of damage per hour to anything you touch.
